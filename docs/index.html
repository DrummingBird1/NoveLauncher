Private by construction

Not a promise in a policy document — an architectural choice.

  • Nothing is collected. No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting in shipped builds, no account, no ads.
  • Usage data stays local. App usage is read from Android and processed on your device; what suggestions learn stays in a local database.
  • Four features use the network — nothing else does. Weather asks Open-Meteo for the current conditions, sending approximate coordinates only if you grant location (a default city otherwise). The News page fetches the RSS feeds you enable. Backups go to your own NAS, only if you choose one. And an update check, off by default, asks GitHub at most once a day whether a new release exists.
  • Your backups are yours. On the phone or on your own NAS, optionally encrypted with a password only you hold.
  • Helpers that can't snoop. Jewish-calendar times are calculated on the phone. The optional gestures accessibility service performs two actions — screen off, open notifications — and cannot read your screen.
  • Auditable. The full source is published so you can verify all of the above yourself.
On app lock, honestly: the app lock, private folder and hidden apps are a deterrent, not a sandbox. A locked app is still reachable from recents, notifications, another launcher or ADB — true of every third-party launcher. For real isolation, use Android's built-in Work Profile or Private Space (Android 15+) — both supported by NoveLauncher.
